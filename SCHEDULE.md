# Road to 100K Weekly Schedule

October 8, 2026

## How the schedule works

The week is 19.5 hours: 15 at the tables, 3 studying, 1.5 on the episode, with Wednesday and Sunday off from play. It assumes work starts at 5:00 pm, so play and study sit in the morning and finish by early afternoon, in online tournaments at a $5 average buy-in, with one episode uploaded every Sunday.

- The play-to-study ratio is about 5 to 1. At $5 buy-ins, volume plus reviewing your own hands beats theory, so study stays short and specific.
- Play two to four tables per block. A bust-out is not the end of a session; the block is.
- Every tournament is picked by expected finish time: turbos, or regular speed with small fields, registered between 9:00 and 11:00 am. Prize pool comes second.
- Two blocks a week are recorded for the episode (Friday and Saturday). The weekday blocks are volume only, which keeps editing to one VOD drop per week.
- Move blocks, not totals. If Tuesday falls through, the hours go to another day or are skipped, never added to an existing block.
- When the tracker raises the average buy-in, keep the hours and keep the stop-loss in buy-ins, not dollars.

## Session routine

Every session follows the same three steps, so the only variable is the cards.

**Before (5 minutes)**

- [ ] Open the tracker and check the suggested average buy-in and the single-tournament cap. Register inside both, not above.
- [ ] Write one focus for the session in the notes field before dealing in (for example "no limping, no calling 3-bets out of position").
- [ ] Start the recording and note the stream clock time if you are playing live.
- [ ] Phone face down, water on the desk, one tab open: the table.

**During**

- Stop-loss is 6 buy-ins per block, re-entries counted. Hit it and there is no more registering, whatever the time is.
- No registration after 11:00 am, late registration included. A run that ends after 2:30 pm is a registration mistake to note, not a reason to skip tomorrow.
- Five-minute break every hour. Stand up, no hand reviews during the break.
- After any hand worth showing, say "clip that" on mic. That is the marker I cut from.
- Quit early if you are tilted, tired, or playing hands your focus note says not to play. Quitting early is not a lost session.

**After (10 minutes)**

- [ ] Log the block as one tournament session in the tracker before you close the client: total buy-ins, total cashes, block hours, episode number, and the events played in the notes.
- [ ] Add two or three hands to the notes with the clock time. These become Monday's review and the episode's spine.
- [ ] Note one thing that went well and one leak in a sentence each.

## Study plan

Study is split in two: Monday reviews your own hands, Wednesday works one topic from a six-week rotation. When the rotation ends, start it again at the next buy-in level, where each topic looks different.

**Monday hand review (1 hour)**

1. Pull the hands you noted after Friday and Saturday's sessions.
2. For each hand, write the villain's likely range on the flop, then the turn, then the river, before judging your line.
3. Pick the single biggest mistake of the week. That becomes Wednesday's warm-up and next week's session focus.

**Wednesday topic rotation (1.5 hours)**

| Week | Topic | What to finish with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Preflop by stack depth: open, 3-bet and defend ranges at 40, 25 and 15 big blinds with antes | Three range charts you can play from memory |
| 2 | Push-fold under 20 big blinds: shove and call ranges by position, with and without antes | A push-fold chart on the desk and a rule for when to stop open-raising |
| 3 | Early levels deep: c-betting and pot control at 100 big blinds, avoiding big pots without big hands | A rule for each board texture and a list of hands you will not stack off with |
| 4 | ICM and the bubble: when folding a chip-EV call is right, laddering versus playing for the win | A written bubble plan for big, medium and short stacks |
| 5 | Final table and heads-up: big-stack pressure, short-stack survival, heads-up ranges | A final table checklist and a heads-up opening range |
| 6 | Game selection and discipline: field size, structure, late registration, re-entry rules and tilt | A list of the tournaments you will play each weekday and a written re-entry rule |

Sources to work from, in order of value: your own hand histories, your poker site's hand replayer, a solver's free tier for a handful of spots per week, and one training video per topic. Watching more than one video per topic is entertainment, not study.

## Sunday review

Forty-five minutes in the tracker turns the week into the episode and next week's plan.

1. Read the week off the tracker: profit, hours, hourly rate, and winning-session rate. Write the four numbers at the top of the episode description.
2. Check the suggested average buy-in. If the bankroll crossed a rung either way, next week's registrations move with it.
3. Pick the three hands for the episode from the clip markers: one win, one loss, one decision you are unsure about. The unsure hand is the one viewers comment on.
4. Copy the stat card and the tags from the tracker into the upload.
5. Send me the VOD link and the three clock times. I return the cut, you upload.
6. Set one focus for next week from Monday's biggest-mistake note and put it in the first session's notes field.

## Move-up rules and monthly checkpoints

Stakes follow the bankroll, never the mood. The tracker applies these rules; this is what they mean at the table.

- Average buy-in is 1 percent of the bankroll: $5 at $500, $10 at $1,000, $25 at $2,500, $50 at $5,000, $100 at $10,000.
- No single buy-in above 2 percent of the bankroll, with re-entries counted as new buy-ins.
- Move down when the bankroll drops far enough that the current average buy-in exceeds 1.5 percent. At a $5 average that is $333. That is the system working, not a failure.
- Downswings of 50 buy-ins are normal in big-field tournaments. The 100 buy-in rule is the minimum, not a cushion; smaller fields and turbos lower the variance at the cost of some return.
- No shots. A shot at a bigger buy-in is a bankroll challenge's most common way to end, and it makes a worse episode than a grind.
