# Road to 100K Weekly Schedule

October 8, 2026

## How the schedule works

The week is 18.5 hours: 14 at the table, 3 studying, 1.5 on the episode, with Wednesday and Sunday off from play. It assumes work starts at 5:00 pm, so play and study sit in the morning and finish by early afternoon, online at NL10 and $5 tournaments, with one episode uploaded every Sunday.

- The play-to-study ratio is about 4 to 1. At micro stakes, volume plus reviewing your own hands beats theory, so study stays short and specific.
- Two sessions a week are recorded for the episode (Friday and Saturday). The weekday sessions are volume only, which keeps editing to one VOD drop per week.
- Move blocks, not totals. If Tuesday falls through, the 2.5 hours go to another day or are skipped, never added to an existing session.
- The schedule changes when the stakes change. When the tracker's ladder moves you up, keep the hours and raise the stop-loss in buy-ins, not in dollars.

## Session routine

Every session follows the same three steps, so the only variable is the cards.

**Before (5 minutes)**

- [ ] Open the tracker and check the suggested stake. Play that stake, not the one you feel like.
- [ ] Write one focus for the session in the notes field before dealing in (for example "no limping, no calling 3-bets out of position").
- [ ] Start the recording and note the stream clock time if you are playing live.
- [ ] Phone face down, water on the desk, one tab open: the table.

**During**

- Stop-loss is 3 buy-ins. Hit it and the session is over, whatever the time is.
- Hard cap of 4 hours. Micro stakes reward volume, but your win rate drops before you notice it.
- Five-minute break every hour. Stand up, no hand reviews during the break.
- After any hand worth showing, say "clip that" on mic. That is the marker I cut from.
- Quit early if you are tilted, tired, or playing hands your focus note says not to play. Quitting early is not a lost session.

**After (10 minutes)**

- [ ] Log the session in the tracker before you close the client: buy-in, cash-out, hours, episode number.
- [ ] Add two or three hands to the notes with the clock time. These become Monday's review and the episode's spine.
- [ ] Note one thing that went well and one leak in a sentence each.

## Study plan

Study is split in two: Monday reviews your own hands, Wednesday works one topic from a six-week rotation. When the rotation ends, start it again at the next stake, where each topic looks different.

**Monday hand review (1 hour)**

1. Pull the hands you noted after Friday and Saturday's sessions.
2. For each hand, write the villain's likely range on the flop, then the turn, then the river, before judging your line.
3. Pick the single biggest mistake of the week. That becomes Wednesday's warm-up and next week's session focus.

**Wednesday topic rotation (1.5 hours)**

| Week | Topic | What to finish with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Preflop ranges by position: open-raise, 3-bet, call-3-bet for 6-max | Charts memorized well enough to play without looking |
| 2 | Continuation betting in single-raised pots: when to bet small, big, or check | A rule for each board texture in and out of position |
| 3 | Micro stakes population: limpers, short stacks, calling stations | A list of three exploits you will use and three bluffs you will stop making |
| 4 | 3-bet pots: stack-to-pot ratio and which hands continue | A continuing range for the three most common 3-bet pot boards |
| 5 | Turn and river: thin value bets and bluff-catching against players who rarely bluff big | A value-bet threshold for the river at your stake |
| 6 | Tilt, stop-losses, and tournament basics: push-fold ranges under 15 big blinds if you play MTTs | A written tilt plan and a push-fold chart on the desk |

Sources to work from, in order of value: your own hand histories, your poker site's hand replayer, a solver's free tier for a handful of spots per week, and one training video per topic. Watching more than one video per topic is entertainment, not study.

## Sunday review

Forty-five minutes in the tracker turns the week into the episode and next week's plan.

1. Read the week off the tracker: profit, hours, hourly rate, and winning-session rate. Write the four numbers at the top of the episode description.
2. Check the stakes ladder. If the bankroll crossed a rung either way, next week's sessions are at the new stake.
3. Pick the three hands for the episode from the clip markers: one win, one loss, one decision you are unsure about. The unsure hand is the one viewers comment on.
4. Copy the stat card and the tags from the tracker into the upload.
5. Send me the VOD link and the three clock times. I return the cut, you upload.
6. Set one focus for next week from Monday's biggest-mistake note and put it in the first session's notes field.

## Move-up rules and monthly checkpoints

Stakes follow the bankroll, never the mood. The tracker applies these rules; this is what they mean at the table.

- Move up in cash when the bankroll holds 25 max buy-ins of the next stake: NL25 at $625, NL50 at $1,250, NL100 at $2,500, $1/$2 at $5,000.
- Move down when the bankroll drops below 20 buy-ins of the current stake. At NL25 that is $500, so the first move-up can reverse. That is the system working, not a failure.
- Tournaments: average buy-in is 1 percent of the bankroll, and a single buy-in never exceeds 2 percent.
- No shots. A shot at the next stake is a bankroll challenge's most common way to end, and it makes a worse episode than a grind.

| Every month after | First Sunday | Bankroll above the previous checkpoint | Re-read the session routine and check the stop-loss was honored every time |
